=== 2. What each step down in frame rate actually buys ===

                 total mA     days   gain on the last
51 fps               9.52       13   
30 fps               6.61       19   1.44x
20 fps               5.22       24   1.26x
10 fps               3.83       32   1.35x
5 fps                3.14       39   1.22x
2 fps                2.72       45   1.15x
1 fps                2.59       47   1.05x
0.25 fps             2.48       49   1.04x

The returns stop, and they stop early. 51 -> 10 fps multiplies
life by 2.5x; 10 -> 1 fps by 1.5x; 1 -> 0.25 fps by 1.04x. Below
a few frames a second there is almost nothing left to win, because
the MCU is asleep 99.6% of the time and you are paying its idle
current whatever you do. Which is section 2b.

=== 2b. The same table on a board that can actually sleep ===

This is the finding. On a stock Pico 2 the idle current is 2.4 mA,
so the panel spends its life paying that whatever the frame rate
is, and frame rate is only a 4x lever end to end. Get the idle
down to 0.27 mA and the same span becomes a 20x lever.

             stock Pico 2  tuned board   ratio
51 fps              13 d         16 d   1.2x
30 fps              19 d         26 d   1.4x
20 fps              24 d         38 d   1.6x
15 fps              27 d         48 d   1.8x
10 fps              32 d         68 d   2.1x
5 fps               39 d        113 d   2.9x
2 fps               45 d        188 d   4.2x
1 fps               47 d        242 d   5.2x
0.25 fps            49 d        308 d   6.3x

Until the sleep current is dealt with, dropping the frame rate
is fighting the wrong number.

=== 3. The other levers, all at 20 fps ===

as it stands                                        5.22 mA     24 d   
frame cost halved (better C, or partial update)     4.07 mA     30 d   1.28x
custom board, tuned idle (0.27 mA)                  3.24 mA     38 d   1.61x
both together                                       2.00 mA     60 d   2.61x
...and 10 fps as well                               1.14 mA    102 d   4.57x

Halving the frame cost buys 1.28x and halving the frame rate
buys 1.35x -- the same lever, because duty cycle is frame_ms x fps
and it does not care which factor you shrink. One of them costs
you the motion and the other costs you nothing to look at.

=== 4. The panel is not the problem ===

    1 fps:  MCU   2.55 mA (99%)    panel 0.039 mA (1%)
   10 fps:  MCU   3.58 mA (93%)    panel 0.251 mA (7%)
   20 fps:  MCU   4.73 mA (91%)    panel 0.487 mA (9%)
   51 fps:  MCU   8.30 mA (87%)    panel 1.219 mA (13%)

A reflective LCD has no backlight, so being looked at is free.
Even at its 51 Hz maximum the panel draws about a milliamp, a
twentieth of what the MCU costs to feed it. Every saving worth
having is on the compute side.

The MCU's sleep current decides this and nothing else does. An
ESP32-S3 in light sleep idles ten times lower than an RP2350, and
light sleep keeps RAM -- which this piece needs, because deep
sleep would throw the ecosystem away every frame.

=== 7. What this does not model ===

Regulator loss. A buck-boost at a 2 mA load runs well off its
  efficiency peak; add 15-25% for anything fed from a cell
  through one. An LDO from a single Li-ion cell is often better
  at these currents despite the dropout.
Wake-up cost. Leaving sleep, relocking the PLL and re-enabling
  XIP is tens of microseconds -- nothing at 20 fps, but 5-10% of
  the budget at 0.25 fps where the frames are all that happens.
Panel current on a fully changing image. The vendor figures are
  typical, and every pixel moving every frame will be worse.
Temperature. A cell at 0 C gives perhaps 70% of its rating.
The map screen's 8.4 ms. It is 0.8% of the time and folded into
  the weighted average above; if the cadence ever became
  map-heavy this model would need redoing.
